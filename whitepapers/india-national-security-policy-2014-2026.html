From restraint to resolve: India's national security policy, 2014 to 2026

By Kamlesh Bhagatkar, author & geopolitical analyst

Download PDF (50 pages)

Abstract

In May 2014 a new government took office in New Delhi with a single-party majority, the first in thirty years. This paper asks what that government did with India's national security policy over the twelve years that followed, from 26 May 2014 to November 2026.

The paper argues that the record is a good one, and says so openly: it is advocacy, not a neutral survey. Numbers come from official statements, budget papers and Parliament answers. Where the government's account is disputed, the paper says who disputes it.

Seventeen chapters cover Pakistan and cross-border terrorism, Jammu and Kashmir after Article 370, China and the northern border, border infrastructure, armed forces reform, the defence industry, nuclear and space deterrence, the Indian Ocean, Left Wing Extremism, the Northeast, intelligence and terror finance, cyber and economic security, and diplomacy. Chapter 16 sets out the strongest criticisms and the unfinished work. A timeline and a table of figures follow at the end.
