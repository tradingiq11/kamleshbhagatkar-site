The Uttar Pradesh Turnaround

Yogi Adityanath and the Remaking of a State · by Kamlesh Bhagatkar

Publishers & media: get in touch

For decades Uttar Pradesh was the example people used when they wanted to say that India could not be governed: too big, too poor, too divided, too afraid of its own criminals. In March 2017 a 44-year-old monk in saffron robes took the oath as Chief Minister. He had never been a minister.

The Uttar Pradesh Turnaround argues that what changed the state was one habit, formed over two decades at the Gorakhnath Math and five terms in Parliament: deciding quickly and standing by the decision in public. The book follows that habit through law and order and the end of the mafia era, the expressways, the investment drive, welfare delivery, and Ayodhya, Kashi and the Mahakumbh.

It is told in six parts, from the boy who left home to the leader of a state of about 24 crore people, roughly one Indian in six. The last part asks what that record means beyond Uttar Pradesh.
