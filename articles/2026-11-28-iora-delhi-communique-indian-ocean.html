The IORA Council of Ministers Meets in Delhi: What the Delhi Communiqué Gives Bharat

By Kamlesh Bhagatkar, politician, author & geopolitical analyst

Download PDF

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookLinkedInTelegram

On Friday, 27 November, External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar chaired the 25th meeting of the Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA) Council of Ministers in New Delhi. Ministers and heads of delegation from all 23 member states and 12 dialogue partners attended, according to News On AIR and ANI. The dialogue partners include China, Russia, the United States, Japan, the European Union and the United Kingdom. India holds the IORA chair for 2025 to 2027, with Mauritius as vice-chair.

The meeting ended with the adoption of the Delhi Communiqué 2026. It also endorsed two Indian proposals for IORA centres of excellence: an IORA Digital Innovation Hub at the CSIR-National Institute of Oceanography in Goa, focused on ocean sciences, and an IORA Centre of Excellence in Maritime Law at Rashtriya Raksha University in Gujarat. "Furthermore, reaffirming its commitment to strengthening IORA, India announced a grant of USD 500,000 towards the Association's institutional and financial capacity-building," the Ministry of External Affairs said, as quoted by ANI. Ministers also discussed IORA's next Action Plan, for 2028 to 2032.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi also met the visiting ministers. In a social media post he said India, as IORA chair, would work closely with all partners under its MAHASAGAR vision (Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions), News On AIR reported.

What the ministers signed in Delhi

According to the text quoted by ANI, the communiqué commits members to "maintaining the Indian Ocean as a region of peace, stability and prosperity, and to upholding applicable international law, including applicable international law of the sea to ensure freedom of navigation and overflight, unimpeded commerce, peaceful settlement of disputes and other lawful uses of the sea." The members also said: "We express concern over the undermining of maritime safety and security of vessels and seafarers and call for ensuring their safety and security." ANI tied that concern to the conflicts in West Asia and the Black Sea region.

The communiqué also acknowledges MAHASAGAR and India's chairship theme, "Innovation, Openness, Resilience and Adaptability in the Indian Ocean Region".

In his opening remarks Jaishankar said: "The Indian Ocean is central to global trade, energy security, connectivity and prosperity. It is our collective responsibility to ensure that this region remains peaceful, stable and secure." In the general debate, titled "A Stronger Regional Voice: Positioning IORA in a Changing Global Order", he added: "We are understandably focused on the volatile aspects of a transforming global order, especially conflicts, frictions and disruptions. It is equally imperative to appreciate the promising aspects: deep interdependence, strong globalisation and the benefits of international cooperation."

The Maldives made a similar case. According to the Maldives foreign ministry, Foreign Minister Iruthisham Adam said the association should deliver practical solutions, and spoke of what secure waters mean for the livelihoods of Maldivians.

Why the two centres matter more than the words

IORA has existed since 1997 and works by consensus. In the communiqué members "recognise the continued relevance of the Association's consensus-based, evolutionary and non-intrusive approach." That is honest, and it is also the association's limit. With Iran, Australia, Saudi Arabia and the Maldives at one table, and China, Russia and the US as dialogue partners, a text that 23 members sign cannot name anyone who threatens ships. The concern about seafarers had to be written without a culprit.

So I read the centres of excellence and the grant as the real outcome. Communiqués are rarely remembered by the next meeting. A digital innovation hub at NIO Goa and a maritime law centre at Rashtriya Raksha University are institutions on Indian soil doing work for 23 governments. I expect them to train officials and draw people back to India long after our chairship ends in 2027.

The maritime law centre interests me most. Jaishankar asked members to uphold international law, in particular the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). Freedom of navigation is easy to praise and hard to defend when a smaller state's fishing grounds or sea lanes come under pressure. I suspect many smaller Indian Ocean states have few specialists in the law of the sea. If young lawyers from those countries learn that law in Gujarat, Bharat gains friends who think about the sea the way we do. That is influence built slowly, and I think it lasts.

What Bharat gets from chairing IORA

For India this is part of a wider maritime effort. The same week, the Information Fusion Centre for the Indian Ocean Region (IFC-IOR) in Gurugram held a workshop for IORA member states on maritime information fusion and operational coordination, ANI reported. Sharing information about ship movements is how a navy knows about piracy, smuggling or a drifting tanker before it becomes a crisis. Earlier this month I wrote about India's first Foreign Office talks with Somalia, where piracy was high on the agenda, and about the Quad summit in Manila and its work on sea cables. IORA brings together the countries that live on these waters, and I think it carries less of the great-power framing that follows the Quad.

Jaishankar called this ocean central to trade and energy security, and India sits in its middle. In a crisis the smaller coastal states around it matter to us as much as the big capitals do. IORA gives us one room where all of them sit with us as equals.

What to watch

The first test is the 2028 to 2032 Action Plan, which will take shape while India holds the chair. The second is how quickly the Goa and Gujarat centres get staff, courses and their first foreign students. Then comes the handover to Mauritius, the vice-chair, and whether New Delhi and Port Louis keep these projects moving in 2027.
