Modi and Merz Speak Before the India EU FTA Is Signed. What Bharat Should Watch

By Kamlesh Bhagatkar, politician, author & geopolitical analyst

Download PDF

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookLinkedInTelegram

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz telephoned Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Tuesday, 24 November. According to the Prime Minister's Office, the two leaders reviewed the India-Germany Strategic Partnership and "expressed satisfaction at the sustained momentum in bilateral engagements." They reaffirmed their commitment to closer cooperation in sectors including defence and advanced technologies, and they "noted that the India-EU Free Trade Agreement would unlock several new opportunities for expanding bilateral trade and investment."

The call came three weeks before a date that matters. The India EU free trade agreement, whose negotiations were concluded in New Delhi on 27 January, is likely to be signed in Brussels on 16 December, an official told PTI, during Mr Modi's visit there. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said on 18 November, "We hope to sign it in December 2026," and that India is aiming to bring it into force in the first quarter of 2027.

On the same day as the call, External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar met Olaf Lies, Minister-President of the German state of Lower Saxony. Dr Jaishankar wrote on X that they discussed "the strategic, economic and technological imperative of closer India-Germany and India-EU cooperation in the current turbulent era."

Why Germany's voice counts

India and Germany are marking 75 years of diplomatic relations this year. India TV notes that India was among the first countries to establish ties with the Federal Republic, on 7 March 1951, and that the two governments have held Intergovernmental Consultations at the level of heads of government since 2011. The previous Modi-Merz call, according to the same report, was on 10 September.

I read the call as Berlin putting its weight behind the signing. The trade agreement is with the European Union as a whole, and it still has to pass through Brussels. According to the European Commission, as reported by Open magazine, it needs approval by the Council of the EU, signature by both sides, consent from the European Parliament and a final Council decision. India must complete its own ratification. Germany is one voice among 27 in the Council, but on industry and trade I think it is the one other members listen to most. A German Chancellor saying publicly, three weeks before the signing, that the deal will "unlock" opportunities is useful to India.

Germany's Ambassador in New Delhi, Jasper Wieck, put it more directly on 4 November. He said the agreement "is expected to enter into force in the first half of next year" and "will be a game changer for our bilateral trade and investment partnership," adding: "I think we have the potential to integrate our supply chains."

What the agreement gives Bharat

The Commerce Ministry's release of 27 January has the numbers. In 2024-25 India's trade in goods with the EU was USD 136.54 billion: exports of USD 75.85 billion and imports of USD 60.68 billion. Services trade was USD 83.10 billion in 2024. The ministry says more than 99% of Indian exports by trade value will get preferential entry into the EU. Open magazine, citing the ministry's factsheet, reports that 70.4% of tariff lines, covering 90.7% of India's exports, will see duties removed as soon as the agreement enters into force, with the rest phased out over three to five years or given quotas.

For ordinary families the most important line in the release concerns jobs. Tariffs of up to 10% on almost USD 33 billion of exports from labour-intensive sectors (textiles, apparel, leather, footwear, marine products, gems and jewellery, handicrafts) fall to zero on entry into force. The ministry says this will help workers, artisans, women, youth and MSMEs. These are the sectors where a tariff cut turns most directly into jobs, and if the agreement does one thing well, I think it should be this.

The government also says it has kept dairy, cereals, poultry, soymeal and certain fruits and vegetables protected. That matters to every farming district, including ours in Vidarbha. On cars, the ministry describes a "calibrated and carefully crafted quota based" opening that lets European carmakers bring models into India in higher price bands. The ministry says it also opens the EU market to cars made in India.

The release counts the EU as India's 22nd FTA partner. With the UK and EFTA agreements already done, the ministry argues the deal "effectively opens up the entire European market." I wrote last week about India's talks with Egypt on a trade pact; Europe is a far larger prize.

What Bharat must guard

Two things worry me, and neither is a reason to slow down.

The first is the EU's carbon border mechanism, known as CBAM. The Commerce Ministry says India secured "a forward-looking most-favoured nation assurance extending flexibilities if any granted to third countries," along with technical cooperation on carbon prices and support to cut emissions. Those are promises of process. Whether they protect an Indian exporter will depend on how Brussels applies them, and Indian negotiators should keep pressing.

The second is time. Negotiations ended in January, and the European Parliament's consent is still ahead. When the big powers are making their own arrangements, as they did at the Shenzhen meeting of Trump, Xi and Putin, I think India gains from locking in a rules-based trade bargain with Europe early. A long gap between signing and entry into force would let other pressures creep in.

What to watch

The first date is 16 December in Brussels. An official has called it likely, and the EU's Ambassador to India has said the agreement will be signed during Mr Modi's December visit and come into force next year. After that, I will watch for the European Parliament vote, the start of India's ratification, and the first CBAM talks under the new agreement. For Germany itself, the PMO statement named defence and advanced technologies but gave no project or figure. The next Intergovernmental Consultations will show whether Berlin turns its warm words into specific defence and technology work with India.
