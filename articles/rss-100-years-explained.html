The RSS at 100: What the Sangh Is and How It Works

By Kamlesh Bhagatkar, politician, author & geopolitical analyst

Download PDF

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookLinkedInTelegram

The Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) is a volunteer organisation founded in Nagpur in 1925 by Dr Keshav Baliram Hedgewar. Its basic unit is the shakha, a one-hour daily gathering of volunteers called swayamsevaks, and it has no formal membership. It completed 100 years on Vijayadashami, 2 October 2025, and by its own count it was running 99,423 daily shakhas in November 2026.

I live in Nagpur, and my first book, RSS: 100 Years of Selfless Service (2025), is about the Sangh. I am sympathetic to it. Where a sentence below is my opinion, I say so.

Who founded the RSS, and when?

The RSS's own FAQ says it was founded by Dr Hedgewar "on the auspicious occasion of Vijayadashami in the year 1925 at Nagpur". He was born in Nagpur on 1 April 1889 and returned to the city in 1916 as a qualified doctor, according to the RSS's history page.

A Press Trust of India (PTI) timeline carried by Deccan Herald gives the day of the launch as 27 September 1925. The organisation got its name on 17 April 1926, by a vote among a small group of Hedgewar's followers. The first daily shakha started on 28 May 1926 at the Mohitewada ground in Nagpur.

How is the RSS organised?

There is no enrolment. "There is no fees, no registration form, and no formal application," the FAQ says. A person who starts attending a shakha is a swayamsevak. The FAQ says "any Hindu male can become a Swayamsevak", and that women can join the Rashtra Sevika Samiti, which Lakshmibai Kelkar of Wardha started in 1936 after consulting Dr Hedgewar.

A pracharak is a swayamsevak who gives his full time to the work. The Sarsanghchalak is the topmost leader, described by the RSS as "Friend, Philosopher and Guide". The Sarkaryavah, or general secretary, is the executive head. Dattatreya Hosabale was elected to that post in 2021, according to the PTI timeline.

The Akhil Bharatiya Pratinidhi Sabha is the annual national meeting and the highest decision-making body. It first met in March 1950. In March 2026 it met at Samalkha in Haryana, where Hosabale said 1,487 representatives were expected and that they are elected or nominated for three-year terms, as Organiser reported. A smaller body, the Akhil Bharatiya Karyakari Mandal, meets every year between Dussehra and Diwali.

The RSS divided the country into 46 prants, or provinces, until 4 November 2026, when it announced that "instead of 46 prants, there will be 88 Sambhags". The headquarters are in Nagpur. Money comes from gurudakshina, an offering each swayamsevak makes once a year before the saffron flag.

The RSS publishes shakha counts and no membership figure, because "the RSS does not maintain a record of sorts for the swayamsevaks". Its figures on 4 November 2026 were:

  • 99,423 daily shakhas at 63,208 places, which is 12,025 more shakhas than a year earlier
  • 34,982 weekly gatherings (milans) and 14,096 monthly ones (mandalis)
  • 1,03,397 places with direct Sangh work, against 93,156 a year earlier

In March 2017 the same FAQ counted 57,185 daily shakhas.

Who have been the six Sarsanghchalaks?

  • Dr Keshav Baliram Hedgewar, the founder, formally designated Sarsanghchalak in November 1929. He died on 21 June 1940.
  • Madhav Sadashiv Golwalkar, known as Guruji, from 3 July 1940 until his death on 5 June 1973.
  • Madhukar Dattatraya Deoras, known as Balasaheb, from 6 June 1973.
  • Rajendra Singh, known as Rajju Bhaiya, from 11 March 1994.
  • K. S. Sudarshan, from 11 March 2000.
  • Dr Mohan Bhagwat, named by Sudarshan as his successor in 2009 and still in the post.

The dates are from the PTI timeline.

What happens at a shakha?

The RSS describes it as a daily gathering of swayamsevaks of different ages at a fixed ground for one hour. The routine is "physical exercises, singing patriotic chorus, group discussions on varied range of subjects and a prayer for our motherland". The saffron flag, the Bhagwa Dhwaj, is treated as the guru.

Reporters from the news agency AFP, who attended a shakha in Nagpur in October 2025, saw dozens of members, many of them middle-aged or elderly and not in uniform, gather for an hour of calisthenics and song.

I wrote about what this routine builds over the years in A Hundred Years of Sewa.

What is sewa, and how large is it?

Sewa means service. In Jaipur on 26 October 2026, Hosabale said organisations affiliated to Seva Bharati were carrying out around 1.5 lakh social service activities across the country, PTI reported.

The RSS also reports relief work after disasters. In its statement of 4 November 2026 it said more than 1,600 swayamsevaks had helped 42,200 families with food, water and medicines in the flood-hit districts of Sivasagar and Jorhat in Assam, with more than Rs 5 crore collected for the work.

These are the organisation's own numbers. I have not found an independent count.

What is the Sangh Parivar?

It is the common name for the family of organisations started by swayamsevaks. The RSS's position is that it "per se" only runs shakhas and training camps. It quotes Dr Hedgewar's comparison: "the RSS is like a powerhouse that generates electric power and transmits it to various establishments". Trained swayamsevaks, it says, launch their own initiatives, and in 2017 it counted more than 40 such organisations at the national level.

The PTI timeline lists some of them with their founding years: the student body Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (1949), the Vanvasi Kalyan Ashram (1952), the trade union Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh (1955), the Vishva Hindu Parishad (1964), the Bharatiya Janata Party (1980), Sanskar Bharati (1981) and Laghu Udyog Bharati (1994). The scholar Christophe Jaffrelot, writing for the Carnegie Endowment in December 2014, added the Bharatiya Kisan Sangh, the Swadeshi Jagaran Manch and Sahakar Bharati.

When was the RSS banned, and how did each ban end?

Three times.

The first ban came in February 1948, after the assassination of Mahatma Gandhi on 30 January 1948. The government's communique said the step was taken "to root out the forces of hate and violence that are at work in our country and imperil the freedom of the nation and darken her fair name", as quoted by Deccan Herald. Golwalkar and thousands of swayamsevaks were arrested. The government lifted the ban in July 1949 (the PTI timeline says 12 July), and the Sangh's constitution was drafted that year.

The second ban came on 4 July 1975, days after the Emergency was imposed on 25 June 1975. Deoras was arrested. The ban was lifted on 22 March 1977.

The third came on 10 December 1992, after the demolition of the Babri mosque in Ayodhya. The Bahri Tribunal found the ban unjustified, and it was lifted on 4 June 1993.

How was the centenary marked?

On 1 October 2025, Prime Minister Narendra Modi was chief guest at a centenary function at the Dr Ambedkar International Centre in New Delhi, where he released a commemorative postage stamp and a Rs 100 coin. According to the Prime Minister's Office, the coin carries the national emblem on one side and Bharat Mata with a lion, saluted by swayamsevaks, on the other. The stamp recalls swayamsevaks marching in the Republic Day parade of 1963.

The next day, 2 October 2025, Dr Bhagwat gave the annual Vijayadashami address in Nagpur, with former President Ram Nath Kovind present. The centenary year ran until Vijayadashami 2026. The RSS says that in that year its workers visited 10,22,05,665 homes and held 65,664 Hindu Sammelans attended by 4,93,76,694 people.

What do critics say, and how does the RSS answer?

AFP reported in October 2025 that critics accuse the RSS of "eroding the rights of India's Muslim minority and undermining the secular constitution". The historian Mridula Mukherjee told the agency that archives showed "a link between the RSS and fascist movements in Europe". The scholar Ram Puniyani told Outlook: "They now say Hindutva welcomes everyone, but you must first enter the lanes of Hindu Rashtra."

The RSS rejects these descriptions. "Those allegations are baseless," Dr Bhagwat told AFP. "Atrocities were never done by the RSS. And if it happens anyway, I condemn that." The RSS FAQ says it uses "Hindu" for the national identity of the people of this country: "It is not a religious or sectarian identity." It adds that Christians and Muslims can become swayamsevaks if they accept that view. In his address of 2 October 2025, Dr Bhagwat said of Indians who follow religions that came from outside: "According to Bharatiya traditions, all are welcome and accepted."

My own view is that an organisation should be judged by what its ordinary volunteers do, and what I have seen of them in Nagpur is discipline and service. The critics deserve to be read in their own words, which is why I have quoted them.

Where does the RSS stand in November 2026?

The centenary year is over, the shakha count is close to one lakh, and the organisation is changing its map from 46 prants to 88 sambhags. Asked on 4 November 2026 about the registration of the Sangh, Hosabale replied that the "Sangh is working within constitutional arrangements".

The thing to watch is the next annual report at the Pratinidhi Sabha. It will show whether the 12,025 shakhas added in the centenary year have lasted, and I think that will say more about the year than the totals do.
