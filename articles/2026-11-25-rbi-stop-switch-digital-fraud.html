RBI's Stop Switch and Trusted Person Plan: More Control Over Your Bank Account

By Kamlesh Bhagatkar, politician, author & geopolitical analyst

Download PDF

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookLinkedInTelegram

The Reserve Bank of India has proposed a Stop Switch for bank accounts. Under draft directions released on 23 November, every bank would have to give customers a way to block, at once, all future outgoing debits from their accounts. "A bank shall provide its customers with a Stop Switch facility for immediate disablement of all outward future debits associated with her / his account(s)," the draft says, as quoted by ANI. Incoming money would still arrive.

The same drafts propose limits that customers set for themselves, digital channels switched off by default for new accounts, and an optional "trusted person" who must approve large transfers. The RBI has issued seven sets of draft amendment directions, one each for commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban co-operative banks and rural co-operative banks. Comments are open until 14 December 2026.

The RBI's directions of 24 June on fraudulent electronic banking transactions dealt mainly with who bears the loss after a fraud. Now, it says, "a need is felt to strengthen the same from a preventive perspective by providing customers with greater control over the manner and extent to which digital payment channels may be used."

What the draft proposes

Customers could switch off one or more digital payment channels linked to an account and switch them back on later, through a branch or a digital interface. They could set a maximum amount per transaction, per beneficiary and per day for each channel, an overall daily limit across all channels, and a time window when chosen channels stay off. The RBI gives an example in the draft, reported by MediaNama: a customer sets a limit of Rs 20,000 a day for payments to one beneficiary, and payments to that person cannot cross it even if the channel itself allows more.

For cards, customers would be able to turn domestic and international use on or off, and set limits for ATMs, PoS terminals, online and contactless payments.

The Stop Switch goes further. Once it is on, Business Standard reports, the customer cannot start any debit, create a new payment mandate or standing instruction, or register the account on a new third-party UPI app. Existing recurring mandates and standing instructions would continue, as would debits by the bank itself to recover charges or loan instalments.

New accounts would start with digital channels disabled. They would open only after explicit consent and authentication. New physical and virtual cards would work only for contact-based use at ATMs and PoS terminals in India until the customer changes that. For the first 24 hours after a channel is switched on, the bank would set a risk-based ceiling on transfers.

The trusted person

This is the part I expect families to talk about. A customer could register a trusted person, aged at least 18, who must approve any transfer above a threshold the customer chooses. The trusted person gets the amount, beneficiary and narration, and has one hour to approve. If they reject it or do not reply, the transaction fails. There is a 24-hour cooling-off period before the mechanism starts.

The draft is careful about what this person is not. Registration does not make them a joint holder, nominee or authorised signatory, gives them no right in the account, and does not let them see the balance, deposits, investments or insurance details. Their mobile number must differ from the one linked to the account. The mechanism is voluntary and needs consent from both people.

It would not apply to UPI payments from the linked account, merchant payments, or recurring payments through standing instructions, e-mandates or NACH. Business Standard also notes that two ideas from the RBI's April discussion paper, a one-hour delay on some high-value payments and an annual credit ceiling, are not in this draft.

MediaNama reads the trusted person idea as a possible safeguard for elderly account holders who are not comfortable with technology and may fall for social engineering. I think of my own elders when I read this. A son or daughter who must approve a large transfer within an hour gives the family time to ask questions before the money is gone. I would still like the RBI to explain why UPI payments are outside the mechanism. MediaNama notes that the draft gives no reason for the exclusions.

The bank's side of the bargain

The draft also widens what counts as "negligence by a bank." According to ANI and MediaNama, the list includes failing to apply the required authentication, failing to send mandatory transaction alerts, not offering 24-hour fraud reporting, not acting promptly on a complaint, not providing the new customer controls or not applying them, system malfunctions or security breaches, and collusion by bank staff or third parties the bank has engaged. MediaNama points out that the list begins with "including", so it is not exhaustive.

For me this is the most important change. Customers who are careful should not carry the loss when the bank's own systems fail. If a customer has set a limit and the bank lets a transfer through anyway, that should be the bank's problem, and under this draft it would be.

Fraud is also being tackled from the telecom side. I wrote recently about SIM binding for messaging apps, which starts on 1 January. The RBI draft covers the other end, the moment the money leaves the account.

Dates to watch

For commercial banks, ANI reports, the customer controls and the trusted person mechanism would generally apply from 1 July 2027, earlier if a bank chooses, while the wider negligence definition would start on 1 January 2027. Banks must offer the controls through branches, ATMs, internet and mobile banking and IVR, notify customers of every change and keep tamper-proof logs. The next date is 14 December, the last day for public comments. Readers with a view on the UPI exclusion can write to the RBI before then.
