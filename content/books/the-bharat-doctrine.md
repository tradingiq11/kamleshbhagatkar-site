In May 2014 India's foreign policy was known for its caution. Twelve years later the same country had struck back across the Line of Control, held its ground at Doklam and Galwan, bought Russian oil while deepening its partnership with America, brought the African Union into the G20 and flown its citizens home from Yemen, Kabul, Ukraine and Sudan.

*The Bharat Doctrine* tells how that change was made. It follows the story from the swearing-in of 26 May 2014 to November 2026, through the neighbourhood, the great powers and the Global South, and sets out the idea beneath it: interest first, firm with adversaries, generous with neighbours. A partner to every great power, a client of none.

The book draws on official records, treaty texts, parliamentary debates and public speeches, and is sourced throughout in the Notes. It makes the case for India's foreign policy plainly.
