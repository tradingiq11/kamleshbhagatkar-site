The Quad is a grouping of four countries: India, the United States, Japan and Australia. It has no treaty and no secretariat, and it is not a military alliance. Its work is practical cooperation in the Indo-Pacific, mostly at sea and in supply chains, and I think India gains more from it than it gives up.

The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) describes the four as "vibrant democracies, market economies, and pluralistic societies" with a shared commitment to "an open, free, and inclusive Indo-Pacific that is prosperous and resilient".

## Where did the Quad come from?

It began with a disaster. According to the MEA's brief on the Quad, the four countries first came together during the 2004 tsunami in the Indian Ocean, to coordinate their relief work.

The first meeting of Quad officials took place in Manila in May 2007, on the sidelines of an ASEAN Regional Forum meeting. Then the grouping went quiet. The MEA brief records "a gap of 10 years" before senior officials met again in 2017, to discuss connectivity, maritime security and counter-terrorism.

The foreign ministers met for the first time in New York on 26 September 2019. The first Quad Leaders' Summit was held online on 12 March 2021, convened by the United States.

## How many summits have there been?

Seven, by the MEA's count. After the online meeting of 12 March 2021, the leaders met in person in Washington on 24 September 2021, online again on 3 March 2022 to discuss the conflict in Ukraine, in Tokyo on 24 May 2022, in Hiroshima on 20 May 2023 and in Wilmington, Delaware, on 21 September 2024.

Then came a long gap. The Wilmington statement said the next summit would be "hosted by India in 2025". That did not happen. The seventh summit was held on 17 November 2026 in Manila, hosted by US President Donald Trump. I wrote about the run-up in [Modi Goes to Manila](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-14-modi-manila-asean-quad-test.html) and about the result in [The Quad Summit in Manila](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-18-quad-manila-summit-sea-cables.html).

The foreign ministers meet more often. The MEA counted thirteen meetings between 2019 and 31 October 2026, three of them in 2026: New Delhi on 26 May, Manila on 22 July and Atlanta on 31 October.

## Is the Quad a military alliance?

No. The clearest answer I have found came from External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar at the Munich Security Conference in February 2022, as reported by Business Today:

> I would urge you not to slip into that lazy analogy of an Asian-NATO. It isn't because there are three countries who are treaty allies. We are not a treaty ally. It doesn't have a treaty, a structure, a secretariat, it's a kind of 21st century way of responding to a more diversified, dispersed world.

The MEA's own wording is that the Quad is "an agile and flexible grouping". In the Quad statements I went through for this article, I found no promise by any member to defend another.

## What does the Quad actually do?

On 1 July 2025 the foreign ministers arranged the work under four headings: maritime and transnational security, economic prosperity and security, critical and emerging technology, and humanitarian assistance and emergency response. These are the main programmes.

- The Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness, announced in 2022, gives countries in the region near-real-time data on what is moving in their waters. The Wilmington statement said it had helped "well over two dozen countries" track vessels in their exclusive economic zones, and that the data reaches the Information Fusion Centre for the Indian Ocean Region in Gurugram.
- The Manila summit welcomed the Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration. In 2027 the four countries' P-8 and P-3C patrol aircraft are scheduled to fly together in a maritime exercise "for the first time in a Quad context". The MEA says the first exercise will be in Guam in March 2027.
- The coast guards run the Quad-at-Sea Ship Observer Mission. India hosted the second edition from 25 September to 7 October 2026, when the Indian Coast Guard ship Shaunak sailed from Chennai to Perth.
- The Quad Partnership for Cable Connectivity and Resilience, which covers undersea cables, dates from the 2023 summit. The MEA says the partners have committed over $140 million to undersea cable builds in the Pacific since then. In Manila the leaders welcomed an expansion of Australia's contribution to cover electricity cables, and upcoming cable projects in the Indian Ocean.
- The Quad Critical Minerals Initiative was announced on 1 July 2025, and a framework for it followed in New Delhi on 26 May 2026. It covers mining, processing and recycling. The stated aim is to reduce reliance on any single supplier.
- On health, in 2021 and 2022 the Quad delivered more than 400 million COVID-19 vaccine doses to Indo-Pacific countries, according to the Wilmington statement. The Quad Cancer Moonshot of 2024 began with cervical cancer, and India committed $7.5 million worth of testing kits and vaccines.
- A counter-terrorism working group has existed since March 2023. On 1 July 2025 the foreign ministers condemned the Pahalgam attack of 22 April 2025 "in the strongest terms".

The Manila summit added a Quad Business Leaders' Forum, planned for early 2027, and cooperation on cybersecurity and artificial intelligence.

## Is Malabar a Quad exercise?

The same four navies take part, but Malabar is older than the Quad. It began in 1992 as a bilateral exercise between India and the United States. In November 2025 it was held around Guam, and ANI's report from Guam said it was the sixth time all four nations had taken part.

The MEA brief mentions Malabar only as history: a four-nation exercise with Singapore in the Bay of Bengal in 2007. It does not list Malabar among the Quad's programmes. I read that as deliberate. The four navies train together, but under their own flag and their own arrangement, and the Quad label stays on civilian and coast guard work.

## How do China and Russia describe the Quad?

China's line has been steady. On 26 May 2026, after the foreign ministers met in New Delhi, Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said cooperation between nations "should not target any third party", and added: "We oppose the creation of exclusive 'small cliques' and oppose bloc confrontation."

Russia has used harder words. In New Delhi on 6 April 2021, Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said "some have begun to discuss an 'Asian NATO'", according to Deccan Herald. The same report says he had called the Quad "divisive" in December 2020.

India's answer has been to point to the work. At the MEA briefing on 26 May 2026, Additional Secretary Nagaraj Naidu said: "As we always keep saying, our focus within the Quad has been to deliver practical projects."

The New Delhi statement of May 2026 expressed "serious concerns regarding dangerous and coercive actions" in the South China Sea. The Manila statement expressed "grave concerns about economic coercion, including through arbitrary export restrictions". Neither sentence names a country.

## What does India get from it?

In my view, four things.

The first is a better picture of the Indian Ocean. Our Navy and Coast Guard watch a very large sea, and shared surveillance data helps them without tying India to anyone's war plans.

The second is supply chains. India depends on imports for many minerals and components. A four-country effort on mining, processing and recycling gives our industry more than one door to knock on.

The third is diplomatic support on terrorism. Three large democracies have put their names to language on cross-border terrorism and on Pahalgam.

The fourth is standing. India's own ideas now travel through the Quad: the maritime training initiative called MAITRI is India-led, and the 2024 summit adopted India's proposed principles for digital public infrastructure.

## Where does India keep its own line?

India is the only member that is not a treaty ally of the United States, as Jaishankar's Munich remark makes plain. It also keeps its relationship with Russia. In Canberra on 10 October 2022 he said the Quad "is primarily focused on the Indo Pacific", while India and Russia have "a longstanding relationship, a relationship that has certainly served our interest well", The Tribune reported.

The same applies to energy. At Munich on 14 February 2026, asked about Russian oil, he said: "We are very much wedded to strategic autonomy because it is very much a part of our history and our evolution."

I think this is the right balance. India takes part where its interests match those of the other three, which is mostly at sea, and decides the rest alone.

## Where do things stand, and what should we watch?

As of 28 November 2026, the Quad has met at leaders' level once in more than two years, and that meeting was held in a third country on the margins of ASEAN.

Three things would show whether it is gaining or losing strength. One is the patrol aircraft exercise in Guam in March 2027. Another is the business forum in early 2027, which Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri said India is interested in hosting. The third is the next leaders' summit. The Manila statement gives no date or host for it. I would like to see it held in India.
