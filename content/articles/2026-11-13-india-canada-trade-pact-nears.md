Canada's Minister of International Trade, Maninder Sidhu, was in India this week for the third time in three months. On Wednesday, 11 November, he and his delegation met Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal. Goyal called it a "warm and engaging breakfast meeting" in a social media post and wrote: "Exchanged views on the importance of a balanced and mutually beneficial agreement that delivers meaningful market access," Business Today reported.

On Thursday, at the CII Partnership Summit in Visakhapatnam, Sidhu said the sixth round of trade talks with India is under way, according to IANS. Both sides want to finish the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) before Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit next month. Business Today reported that the visit to Canada is expected on 11 and 12 December, that a formal announcement of the deal is likely then, and that the signing is likely later. From Canada the Prime Minister is expected to go to the G20 summit in the United States on 14 and 15 December, and then to Brussels to sign the India-EU free trade agreement.

Canada's High Commissioner in New Delhi, Christopher Cooter, told The Times of India that he was "pretty confident that we're getting close", according to Business Today. He also warned that "the actual ratification would take some time because we have provincial governments that are involved as well."

## How the two countries got here

Three years ago this would have been hard to imagine. Ties effectively collapsed in 2023, when Ottawa accused the Indian government of a deadly campaign against suspected Sikh separatist activists in Canada. New Delhi rejected the accusations as "absurd". Diplomats were expelled and trade negotiations were frozen, Al Jazeera reported.

The change came with Prime Minister Mark Carney. During his visit to New Delhi, the two sides signed the terms of reference for the CEPA on 2 March 2026. Carney's office said that day that Canada and India "will conclude a new Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA)" this year. The visit also produced a CAD 2.6 billion agreement between the Government of India and Saskatoon-based Cameco for nearly 22 million pounds of uranium from 2027 to 2035, and a Strategic Energy Partnership covering LNG, LPG, uranium, solar and hydrogen.

Progress since then has been steady. A Global Affairs Canada release of 21 September said four rounds had been completed. Business Today now counts six rounds since March, covering 16 policy areas. Accounts of the exact count differ: India News Network, also on 12 November, spoke of five rounds completed, and Sidhu himself described the sixth as under way. The same report said negotiators would meet in Ottawa next week, with another meeting possible before the end of November if issues remain.

Cooter's own description is that the relationship has moved "far beyond a reset". He said "big-ticket announcements" are expected during the Modi visit, including in space.

## What it means for Bharat

I see three reasons this deal matters to us, and energy comes first.

Sidhu named energy, uranium, critical minerals and investment as the core pillars, and said Canada aims to become the world's fifth-largest LNG exporter within five years, IANS reported. That ambition lands at a useful moment. On 11 November, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, the speaker of Iran's parliament and its lead negotiator, told Al Jazeera the Strait of Hormuz would "never return" to its pre-war status. Every new supplier outside the Gulf gives India more room. Uranium from Cameco, on a contract running to 2035, is the same logic applied to nuclear power.

Second, the trade numbers are modest, which is why there is room to grow. Modi said in March that bilateral trade was nearly $9 billion in 2024-25 and should reach $50 billion by 2030, according to Al Jazeera. Ottawa uses a different measure: Carney's office set a target of $70 billion (Canadian dollars, goods and services) by 2030, and Global Affairs Canada put two-way goods and services trade at CAD 30.4 billion in 2025. Its figures show Canada sold India CAD 3.9 billion in goods in 2025 and bought CAD 9.7 billion, mainly precious stones and metals, machinery and pharmaceuticals. Indian exporters are already ahead. A good agreement should lock in that access.

Third, people. Cooter put the number of Indian students in Canada at roughly 300,000, Business Today reported, and said Ottawa's caps target fraudulent institutions, adding that "the caps are nowhere near being met". Canada is also looking at side agreements to ease business travel. For families in Nagpur or Ludhiana weighing a child's admission abroad, that matters more than tariff lines.

Both countries also have a shared reason to hurry. Al Jazeera noted in March that each wants to diversify trade away from the United States because of US tariff actions. I read Ottawa's urgency as partly a hedge against Washington, and that is fine with me. India has its own reasons to spread its bets.

My caution is about pace. A deal announced in December still has to be signed, and Canada has to take its provinces along before it can ratify. In my view, a durable partnership also depends on how both governments handle the issues that broke ties in 2023, and a trade text cannot settle those on its own.

## What to watch next

The first marker is the Ottawa round next week and whether a further meeting is needed before the end of November. The second is 11 and 12 December: whether Modi and Carney announce a concluded CEPA, and whether the "big-ticket" space and energy announcements Cooter spoke of are real agreements with dates. After that, watch Brussels, where the India-EU agreement is due to be signed on the same tour.
