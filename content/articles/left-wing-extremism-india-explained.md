Left Wing Extremism (LWE) is the Home Ministry's term for the armed Maoist insurgency that began at Naxalbari in 1967 and, by the ministry's count, affected 126 districts in 2014. After a security review completed after 31 March 2026, the ministry lists no district as LWE-affected. What is left is 38 districts kept under watch, thousands of surrendered cadres to resettle, and a development gap that I think will decide whether the peace holds.

## What is Left Wing Extremism, and who are the CPI (Maoist)?

The movement is commonly called Naxalism, after the Naxalbari uprising of 1967 in West Bengal. A Press Information Bureau (PIB) backgrounder of 1 August 2026 says it grew from that uprising, inspired by Maoist ideology.

Its main organisation is the Communist Party of India (Maoist). Home Minister Amit Shah told the Lok Sabha on 30 March 2026 that it was formed in 2004, when the People's War Group and the Maoist Communist Centre merged. In June 2009 the Home Ministry added the party by name to the schedule of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, which lists banned terrorist organisations, Deccan Herald and Business Standard reported.

## How large was the insurgency at its peak?

In the same reply, Shah said a Red Corridor had formed across 12 states, covering 17 per cent of the country's land area. The Home Ministry counts 126 affected districts in 2014, of which 35 were classed as most affected.

The worst year was 2010. The ministry records 1,936 violent incidents and 1,005 deaths of civilians and security personnel that year. On 6 April 2010 a Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) party was ambushed near Chintalnar in Chhattisgarh. The then Home Minister, P. Chidambaram, told the Rajya Sabha: "74 men of the CRPF, one Head Constable of the civil police and one driver of the reinforcement party lost their lives."

## What policy did the government apply?

Police and public order are state subjects, so the Centre supports the states. Its framework was the National Policy and Action Plan to address LWE, approved in 2015. The Home Ministry told the Rajya Sabha on 29 July 2026 that the plan combined security measures, development work and the rights and entitlements of local communities, and gave these totals.

- Security: 663 fortified police stations, and Rs 3,805.04 crore released to states since 2014-15 under the Security Related Expenditure scheme.
- Connectivity: 15,189 km of roads and 9,497 mobile towers.
- Development: about Rs 4,289.20 crore since 2017 under Special Central Assistance, for gaps in public infrastructure.
- Surrender: a grant of Rs 5 lakh for higher-ranked cadres and Rs 2.5 lakh for others, with Rs 10,000 a month for three years of vocational training.

## How did the numbers fall?

The Home Ministry's answers in Parliament give this sequence of affected districts: 126, then 90 in April 2018, 70 in July 2021, 38 in April 2024, 18 in April 2025, 11 in October 2025 (three of them most affected), 8 in December 2025, and none after the review of March and April 2026.

Violence fell alongside. These are the ministry's figures for incidents, and for deaths of civilians and security personnel together.

- 2010: 1,936 incidents, 1,005 deaths
- 2014: 870 incidents, 310 deaths
- 2021: 361 incidents, 147 deaths
- 2024: 375 incidents, 151 deaths
- 2025: 234 incidents, 100 deaths
- 2026, up to 21 July: 33 incidents, 11 deaths

The figures from 2021 onward are from the Rajya Sabha answer of 29 July 2026; an earlier ministry release differs by one for 2024.

## What happened between 2024 and 2026?

Shah set the target of ending Naxalism by 31 March 2026 at a review meeting with state police chiefs in Raipur on 24 August 2024, The Tribune reported.

The Home Ministry told the Lok Sabha in December 2025 that security forces killed 290 Maoists in 2024 and 335 in 2025 up to 1 December, arrested 1,090 and 942, and received 881 and 2,167 surrenders.

Three events stand out in the official record. The first is Operation Black Forest on a hill on the Chhattisgarh-Telangana border, which Shah told the Lok Sabha lasted 21 days and killed more than 30 Maoists.

The second came on 21 May 2025 in Narayanpur district of Chhattisgarh. Shah announced that 27 Maoists had been killed, including the party's general secretary, Nambala Keshava Rao alias Basavaraju. "This is the first time in three decades of Bharat's battle against Naxalism that a general secretary-ranked leader has been neutralized by our forces," he wrote on X.

The third was a run of surrenders. On 16 October 2025 Shah said 258 Naxalites had surrendered in two days, 197 of them in Chhattisgarh and 61 in Maharashtra.

## Was the 31 March 2026 deadline met?

The government says yes. In the PIB's English account of his Lok Sabha reply on 30 March 2026, Shah said: "We had set a target to make the country Naxalism-free by 31 March, and we have achieved it." He put the affected districts at two that day.

On 8 April 2026 the Home Ministry wrote to nine states that "no district in the country falls under the LWE-affected category", PTI reported.

The official wording since then is careful. The PIB backgrounder says India became "effectively free" of Naxalism. The Rajya Sabha answer keeps 37 districts as "Legacy & Thrust" districts and one as a "District of Concern", with surveillance and development work continuing "so as to preclude any possibility of their relapse". PTI identified the District of Concern as West Singhbhum.

## What about Gadchiroli, in Maharashtra?

Gadchiroli is in Vidarbha, my own region. In April 2025 the Home Ministry still listed it among the six most-affected districts in the country.

On 15 October 2025, Akashvani News reported, Politburo member Mallojula Venugopal Rao alias Bhupati surrendered in Gadchiroli with 60 other cadres before Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis, who called it the biggest surrender in the history of the movement in Maharashtra. By December 2025 the ministry's list had no district from Maharashtra.

Deccan Herald, which visited in December 2025, quoted District Collector Avishyant Panda: "Gadchiroli is like any other district today." I hope he is proved right. I wrote in [Watching the World from the Centre of India](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/watching-the-world-from-nagpur.html) that security begins at home, and for Vidarbha home includes Gadchiroli.

## What do critics say, and how has the government replied?

The People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL), in a statement of 27 May 2025, alleged "a disturbing pattern of indiscriminate killings in Bastar since January 2024". It asked that every death in police action be followed by an FIR and a magisterial inquiry, as National Human Rights Commission guidelines require.

The police reject such charges. When the banned party alleged in March 2024 that an encounter in Bijapur had been staged, the Inspector General of Police for Bastar Range, Sundarraj P, told PTI: "It is the typical modus operandi of Naxals to level allegations against the security forces to puncture the credibility of security forces."

To those who asked for talks, Shah's reply in the Lok Sabha was that "dialogue is held only with those who surrender their weapons, but those who fire bullets are answered with bullets". On 1 November 2025 PUCL's Chhattisgarh unit asked that security camps be removed from areas declared free of Naxalism, and that the Forest Rights Act be fully implemented.

My view is that the campaign was necessary, because no state can leave territory and citizens to an armed party. I also think specific complaints should go through the inquiry procedure that already exists. A state that has prevailed loses nothing by checking its own conduct.

## What remains?

The first task is watchfulness in the 38 listed districts. Sundarraj P said on 22 May 2026 that the next challenges were to consolidate the gains and "to de-mine the entire region".

The second is rehabilitation. The PIB backgrounder counts 3,927 surrenders between 2024 and March 2026; Shah gave the Lok Sabha a higher figure of 4,839. Outlook reported on 29 July 2026 that Bastar's Superintendent of Police called rehabilitation a major challenge.

The third is development and rights. The Home Ministry says one-third of the security camps in LWE-free areas are being converted into public service centres, and that 21,26,268 title deeds have been distributed to local communities. Down To Earth reported on 15 May 2026, from Tribal Affairs Ministry data, that Chhattisgarh had received 947,479 forest rights claims and rejected 413,411.

## Where do things stand, and what should we watch?

As of 28 November 2026, the most recent official count I have found is the Rajya Sabha answer of 29 July 2026: no affected district, and 33 incidents in the year up to 21 July. Surrenders continue: IANS and Organiser reported that two cadres gave themselves up to Bijapur police in late November 2026.

I would watch whether West Singhbhum leaves the District of Concern category, whether surrendered cadres find steady work, and whether pending forest rights claims in Bastar move. I expect the last two to matter most.
