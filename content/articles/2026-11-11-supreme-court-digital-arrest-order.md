A Supreme Court order reported on Tuesday, 10 November, puts the fight against "digital arrest" scams on the desks of the companies that carry the calls and the banks that carry the money. The case concerns frauds that use forged documents, and, as LiveLaw reported, WhatsApp's action covered accounts linked to these scams and to impersonation of law enforcement agencies.

The order comes from the court's own case, titled In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents. It was passed after a hearing on 29 October by a bench of Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V Mohana, and later uploaded to the court's website. The court directed WhatsApp LLC and other intermediaries to tell it what measures they propose against digital arrest scams and related cybercrime. "They shall also simultaneously submit their proposals to the I4C," the order says, referring to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre.

Senior Advocate Kapil Sibal, appearing for WhatsApp, told the court that the platform had acted against 9,400 accounts and was developing software to identify suspicious accounts and warn users. He said WhatsApp would file an affidavit setting out its steps. The matter is next listed on 23 November.

## Banks asked to carry the risk

The sharpest part of the order is about banks. The bench said the Reserve Bank of India's regulatory measures must require financial institutions to compensate victims of digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds. The amount must be "not less than the amount lost by the customer" where the institution failed to exercise due diligence in preventing or reporting the fraud.

The court also listed safeguards it expects. One is temporarily suspending "unusual or suspicious transactions inconsistent with the account's past transaction history." Others are automatically informing the nearest police station about such transactions and verifying them physically. According to Telangana Today and ETV Bharat, the Chief Justice asked why banks could not step in when a customer suddenly tried to move a large sum that did not fit their usual pattern, and described banks as a "protective umbrella" for vulnerable customers.

The RBI has to give the court a list of all authorised financial institutions at the next hearing. The court also said it would first examine the RBI's proposed Know Your Customer (KYC) Amendment Directions, 2026. Reports of the hearing say these directions are proposed to be notified by 30 November and to come into force by 31 December.

## Police and the states

The order also reaches every state. Law enforcement agencies in all states and Union Territories must report the status of FIRs registered in digital arrest cases and the investigations that followed, and suggest how to prevent, detect and prosecute these crimes. The Directors General of Police are responsible for compliance. Attorney General R. Venkataramani, for the Centre, told the court that substantial work had been done to improve the detection of suspicious transactions in the banking system.

This builds on earlier steps. According to LiveLaw and The Federal, the court gave directions in February, and in August it asked the RBI to frame a standard procedure for banks to put temporary debit holds on accounts linked to cyber fraud, along with a grievance system and a way to return money to victims. States were told to make their State Cyber Crime Coordination Centres work, and an inter-departmental committee was asked to look at shared liability and compensation.

## What it means for ordinary Indians

These scams work because most of us take a call from the police seriously. In my view, too much of the burden has so far rested on the victim, who learns only after the money has gone that the "officer" was a fraud. I read this order as a shift in where responsibility sits. A bank has the transaction history in front of it. If a retired teacher who has never sent more than a few thousand rupees suddenly tries to move lakhs to an unknown account, the bank is better placed than anyone to pause and ask. The court has now said that RBI rules must make an institution that failed in due diligence pay back at least what the customer lost.

I think the same logic applies to messaging platforms, since fake officers reach their victims through them. Asking WhatsApp and others to put their plans before the court and before I4C makes their role in this a matter of record.

The details still have to be written. The case is being heard, and what the court finally settles will matter more than any early reading, including mine.

## What to watch

The next date is 23 November, when the RBI is to hand over its list of financial institutions. WhatsApp has said it will file an affidavit on its steps, and state police have been asked for reports on their cases. After that, the date to watch is 30 November, when the RBI's KYC amendment directions are expected to be notified.
