Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah arrived in New Delhi on Sunday, 22 November, for a two-day state visit, and India Nepal relations get their first real test under his government today. Shah, 36, came at Prime Minister Narendra Modi's invitation and was received at the airport by Union Minister Jitin Prasada, ANI reported in The Tribune. It is his first visit to India since he took office in March 2026, and his first bilateral visit abroad as Prime Minister.

He has brought a 34-member delegation, including Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle and Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal. On Monday he is due to call on President Droupadi Murmu and hold talks with Mr Modi, first one-on-one and then with delegations, followed by a joint press conference, according to Nepal's Foreign Secretary Amrit Bahadur Rai. Nation Path India reported that the talks will be at Hyderabad House and that Shah met External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar at his hotel on Sunday evening. The MEA describes the relationship as "rooted in historical, cultural and civilisational links and strong people-to-people ties", and places the visit under India's Neighbourhood First policy.

The ground was prepared well in advance. Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri was in Kathmandu on 5 November and called on Shah, with energy, infrastructure and connectivity on the agenda, All India Radio reported.

## What is on the table

Most of the expected outcomes are things people can see and use. The first is a direct passenger train between Janakpur and Ayodhya, which the two prime ministers are due to flag off. Himalaya Post reports that the route covers about 604 km and takes around 19 hours, and that Indian Railways will supply an 18-coach train and run the service for nearly 1,100 passengers. The 28 km section inside Nepal was built entirely with Indian grant assistance. Janakpur is revered as the birthplace of Sita and Ayodhya as the birthplace of Ram. I think a train between the two will do more for goodwill than any speech this week.

The second is power. Reports say the 669 MW Lower Arun hydropower project in eastern Nepal, listed by India's SJVN on its website, will feature in the visit; accounts differ on whether it will be a virtual inauguration or a foundation stone. The Kathmandu Post reports that Nepal wants to sell more than 1,650 MW of power to India and to raise its exports to Bangladesh beyond 40 MW. These are the right ambitions. In January 2024 the two countries signed an agreement that set a target of 10,000 MW of Nepali power exports to India over ten years.

The two sides are also drafting a 10-point joint vision under the slogan "Samriddha Nepal, Samunnat Bharat", to be released with the joint communiqué, the Kathmandu Post reported. A Nepali minister told the paper: "We will set goals, priorities and targets in each specific area so that both sides can see concrete progress." Memorandums on education, health, agriculture, sports, disaster management and human resource development are also expected, along with the Integrated Check Post at Bhairahawa.

## What Kathmandu wants, and where Delhi will be careful

Nepal has bigger requests. The Kathmandu Post reports that it is seeking a financial package of about $2 billion in grants and credit lines. It also wants new air entry routes through Mahendranagar and Bhairahawa, with Nepalgunj and Janakpur raised as well. India has cited security concerns about these routes in the past.

I understand Delhi's caution on air routes, because airspace near a border is a security matter for any country. I also think a firm "no" for years makes it look as if India is blocking Nepal's growth. A phased route with clear security conditions would be a better answer, and I hope the officials look for one.

Several older questions will stay where they are. The Kathmandu Post lists the boundary dispute, Nepal's widening trade deficit, the stalled Pancheshwar multipurpose project and the recruitment of Nepali citizens into the Indian Army, suspended since 2020, as unlikely to see real progress on this visit. That is honest, and it is better to say so than to promise breakthroughs that will not come.

## What it means for Bharat

India and Nepal share an open border, and families on both sides live with that fact every day. When Nepal's economy grows through trade with India, through power sold into our grid and through trains to our pilgrim towns, Nepal's prosperity and India's security point the same way. I read this visit as a chance to make that link stronger and more visible to ordinary Nepalis.

Hydropower matters most. Nepal's rivers flow into ours, and the same Himalaya that worries us on the Siang, as I wrote about [China's Medog dam](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-22-siang-project-china-medog-dam.html) last week, is an opportunity in Nepal. Every megawatt Nepal sells to India or through India to Bangladesh gives Kathmandu a stake in a South Asian grid that runs through Bharat.

A young leader on his first bilateral trip abroad will want something to show at home. India should help him show it. Generosity on trains, power lines and check posts costs us little and earns trust that lasts longer than any single government.

## What to watch

Watch for three documents after today's talks: the joint communiqué, the 10-point vision paper and the list of agreements. The vision paper will matter only if it carries the sector targets the Nepali side has promised. Watch whether the Lower Arun event is an inauguration or a foundation stone, and whether the Janakpur to Ayodhya train actually runs on schedule. And watch the language on air routes and the $2 billion package. If either appears in the communiqué with numbers, this visit will have moved the relationship forward.
