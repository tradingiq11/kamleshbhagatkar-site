The Colombo Security Conclave, the small Indian Ocean security grouping that India helped build, held its 8th meeting of national security advisers in the Maldives on Wednesday, 25 November. The venue was Villa Nautica Paradise Island. National Security Advisor Ajit Doval led India's delegation. According to the joint press statement released by the Ministry of External Affairs on Thursday, member states signed the Agreed Minutes and adopted a Roadmap of Cooperation for 2026-2027.

The other delegations were led by the Maldives' National Security Advisor, Lt. Col. (Retd.) Ibrahim Latheef; Sri Lanka's Defence Secretary, Air Vice Marshal (Retd.) Sampath Thuyacontha; and Lieutenant General Md Faizur Rahman, Commandant of Bangladesh's National Defence College. Mauritius joined virtually. Australia and Malaysia attended as guests.

In his address, Doval said: "For India, the Colombo Security Conclave represents the intersection of our Neighbourhood-First outlook and the MAHASAGAR vision, which stands for Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Areas. We are committed to continue working closely with the CSC countries to enhance our collective capacities in dealing with the evolving serious security challenges."

## What was agreed

The roadmap sets out deliverables and planned activities under the grouping's five pillars: maritime safety and security; countering terrorism and radicalisation; combating trafficking and transnational organised crime; cyber security and protection of critical infrastructure and technology; and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief. The statement says members "placed emphasis on strengthening collaboration across the five pillars of cooperation, particularly through training, sharing best practices and capacity-building initiatives."

The grouping's first Secretary General, Vice Admiral (Retd.) S N Ghormade, presented a review of what had been done to carry out the decisions of the 7th meeting, held in New Delhi on 20 November 2025. Doval welcomed his appointment and, as reported by ANI, described the conclave as having grown into an action-oriented mechanism.

This will not make headlines the way a summit does, and I think the routine is where its value lies. A roadmap with named pillars and a secretary general who reports on what was done last year is how a regional body moves from talk to habit.

## Why a small grouping matters to Bharat

The conclave began in 2020, when India, Sri Lanka and the Maldives widened their trilateral meeting on maritime cooperation, according to The Daily Star. Mauritius joined in March 2022, and Bangladesh was welcomed as the fifth member in 2024. At the New Delhi meeting last year, members welcomed Seychelles' decision to accede as a full member, All India Radio reported.

Look at that list on a map. These are the island and coastal states around India's southern and western seas, the waters our trade and energy cross every day. To my mind, a problem in Malé or Colombo is very quickly a problem in Mumbai or Chennai. I wrote earlier about how the [Quad summit in Manila put sea cables on the agenda](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-18-quad-manila-summit-sea-cables.html). The conclave's pillar on critical infrastructure deals with the same problem closer to home.

The other pillars match the threats our neighbours actually face: trafficking by sea, terrorism and radicalisation, cyber attacks on critical infrastructure, and natural disasters. I do not think a small island state can handle these alone. India has a large navy and coast guard, training institutions and long experience of disaster relief in the region. In my view, a standing grouping gives these countries a regular way to draw on that capacity without each request turning into a bilateral negotiation.

Doval also spoke about history. "We, the littoral states of the Indian Ocean region, share a common maritime space and are connected by civilisational heritage shaped over a long period of history," he said, as reported by ANI. All India Radio quoted him saying "the seas must remain a space that connects nations rather than divides them." I read this as a deliberate reminder that India's ties in this region are old and local, and that outside powers arriving with ports and loans are the newcomers.

## Two details worth noticing

First, the guests. Australia and Malaysia attended, as Malaysia did in New Delhi last year. I see that as a way to widen the conversation towards the eastern Indian Ocean and the Malacca approaches without diluting a membership that is still made up of India's immediate maritime neighbours.

Second, the signatures. The joint statement, as reported by The Shillong Times and Greater Kashmir, names the Maldives, India, Mauritius (virtually) and Sri Lanka as the member states that signed the Agreed Minutes and adopted the roadmap. Bangladesh's delegation took part, led by its National Defence College commandant, but it is not named in that list. Seychelles does not appear in any report of the meeting that I read. The statements I saw give no reason for either. I would not draw a firm conclusion from a press statement, but it is worth asking Dhaka and Victoria directly. I have written recently about the [problems on the Bangladesh border](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-21-bangladesh-border-bsf-attacks-fencing.html). Security cooperation at sea is one area where both countries gain, whatever the politics on land.

## What to watch next

The roadmap is for 2026-27, so the test is whether the training, exchanges of best practice and capacity-building it promises across the five pillars actually take place on time. The Secretary General's review at the 9th meeting will show how much was delivered.

The other thing to watch is membership. If Seychelles takes its place as a full member and Bangladesh appears among the signatories next time, the conclave will cover almost every neighbour that sits on India's western and southern sea lanes. That would give New Delhi a regional security forum where India is the natural anchor and no outside power sits as a member.
