The India-US trade deal is a Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA) that the two governments have been negotiating in stages since February 2025. The first stage, called the interim agreement, has had an agreed framework since 7 February 2026. As of 28 November 2026 its legal text has not been signed, and the American tariff it was built on has been replaced twice.

I think India has bargained sensibly so far. As I read it, New Delhi has kept its sensitive farm and dairy products out and has held back from signing until the American tariff structure is settled.

## What is being negotiated, and who negotiates it?

On 13 February 2025, in Washington, President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced plans to negotiate "the first tranche of a mutually beneficial, multi-sector Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA) by fall of 2025".
At the political level the talks are led by Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, whose office is known as USTR. All India Radio reported on 2 June 2026 that India's chief negotiator is Darpan Jain, and that the US team is led by its chief negotiator, Brendan Lynch.

## How did it start, and what has happened since?

The same leaders' statement of 13 February 2025 set a goal called "Mission 500", which it described as "aiming to more than double total bilateral trade to $500 billion by 2030".

- In April 2025 President Trump announced reciprocal tariffs. India's rate was 26%, and on 9 April he suspended the higher rates for 90 days (US Congressional Research Service, CRS).
- An executive order of 31 July 2025 set India's rate at 25%. A second order, on 6 August 2025, added another 25% on the ground that India was "directly or indirectly importing Russian Federation oil". PTI reported that the combined 50% applied from 27 August 2025.
- Five rounds of talks had been held by then. The sixth, due in New Delhi from 25 August 2025, was postponed, The Tribune reported.
- On 7 February 2026 the two governments issued a joint statement with a framework for the interim agreement. The United States would apply "a reciprocal tariff rate of 18 percent" to Indian goods. An order signed on 6 February 2026 removed the 25% Russian-oil duty from 7 February.
- On 20 February 2026, hours after the US Supreme Court struck down the President's sweeping tariffs, he imposed a 10% surcharge on imports for 150 days from 24 February, under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974.
- Indian negotiators were in Washington from 20 to 23 April 2026, and Mr Greer came to New Delhi from 22 to 24 June 2026.
- On 24 July 2026, when the surcharge expired, USTR imposed tariffs on 60 economies under Section 301 over forced-labour import rules. India is in the 10% group. PTI reports that the duty is over and above normal rates.
- On 25 November 2026 USTR proposed tariffs of up to 25% on Indian goods in a second Section 301 case, on "structural excess capacity", and said it would consider 17% if India signs an agreement. I wrote about that in [The US Proposes a 25% Tariff on India Over "Excess Capacity"](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-26-us-section-301-tariff-india-excess-capacity.html).

## How big is India-US trade, and what does each side sell?

USTR puts total trade in goods and services at an estimated $239.6 billion in 2025. Goods were $149.1 billion: the United States exported $45.4 billion to India and imported $103.8 billion, a deficit of $58.4 billion.

India counts by financial year. Commerce Ministry data reported by ANI show exports to the United States of USD 87.31 billion in 2025-26 and imports of USD 52.90 billion. The United States remained India's top export destination.

CRS lists the main goods India sold to the United States in 2024 as pharmaceuticals and medicines, IT equipment, apparel and textile furnishings. The main American goods sold to India were oil and gas, coal and petroleum gases, aerospace products and basic chemicals.

## What does the United States want?

Under the framework of 7 February 2026, India is to "eliminate or reduce tariffs on all U.S. industrial goods" and on a range of American farm products, including dried distillers' grains and red sorghum for animal feed, tree nuts, fruit, soybean oil, and wine and spirits.

Washington also wants non-tariff barriers removed. India agreed to address barriers to American medical devices and farm products, and to end restrictive import licensing for information and communication technology goods.

Then there are purchases. The statement says India "intends to purchase $500 billion of U.S. energy products, aircraft and aircraft parts, precious metals, technology products, and coking coal over the next 5 years". Digital trade rules are left for the full BTA. On defence, the order of 6 February 2026 records that India had committed to a framework to expand defence cooperation over the next 10 years.

## What does India want?

The first demand is a lower American tariff than competitors pay. On 12 November 2026 Mr Goyal said: "We will have the most preferred market access in the US post the deal in products of our interest." I discussed it in [Goyal Promises "One of the Best" US Trade Deals](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-13-goyal-india-us-trade-deal.html).

The 18% rate in the framework was to cover textiles and apparel, leather and footwear, plastic and rubber, organic chemicals and artisanal products, which are our labour-intensive exports. On conclusion of the interim agreement, the reciprocal tariff was to go altogether on generic pharmaceuticals, gems and diamonds, and aircraft parts.

Steel and aluminium duties come under a separate law, Section 232. ThePrint reported that they were raised to 50% in 2025 and survived the court ruling. The framework removes them only for certain aircraft and aircraft parts, and gives India a preferential quota for automotive parts.

CRS notes that Indian leaders have also raised American temporary visa and Social Security tax policies that affect Indians working in the United States. The framework says nothing on either.

The Russian-oil penalty is gone, conditionally. The American order says India "has committed to stop directly or indirectly importing Russian Federation oil" and tells the Secretary of Commerce to monitor whether imports resume. India puts it differently. At Munich on 14 February 2026, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar said Indian oil companies "look at availability, look at costs, look at risks and take decisions that they feel are in their best interest".

## What are India's red lines on farming and dairy?

On 7 August 2025, the day after the second tariff order, Prime Minister Modi said India would never compromise on the interests of its farmers, fishermen and dairy farmers, All India Radio reported.

On 7 February 2026 Mr Goyal said agricultural and dairy products that India produces adequately, including maize, wheat, rice, sugar, soybean and poultry, had been kept outside tariff concessions. In March 2026 he added, as The Tribune reported, that there would be "no GM products".

I think the line is right. The opening India has given is in animal feed, nuts, fruit and soybean oil, and the fine print on those is what I would read first when the legal text appears.

## What have the tariffs done to Indian exports?

Commerce Ministry data showed exports to the United States falling 11.93% to USD 5.46 billion in September 2025, the first full month of the 50% tariff, and 8.58% to USD 6.3 billion in October 2025.

For the whole of 2025-26 they were USD 87.31 billion, against USD 86.51 billion the year before. Between April and September 2026 they rose 11.1% to USD 50.81 billion. Imports from the United States grew faster, by 26%, and India's surplus narrowed to USD 18.48 billion from USD 20.07 billion.

As I read it, the damage was less than many feared, but the totals hide the pain. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal credited the 2026 growth to electronics, petroleum products, engineering goods, chemicals and agricultural products. The totals do not show how a garment or leather exporter fared.

## Where do things stand, and what should we watch?

As of 28 November 2026 the interim agreement is unsigned. Mr Agrawal gave the reason on 15 October 2026: "The deal has to be signed when the overall tariff structure is getting formalised." On 25 November 2026 the Commerce Ministry rejected the excess-capacity charge and said it remains committed to an early conclusion of the BTA.

The framework has a clause for this situation: if either country changes the agreed tariffs, the other "may modify its commitments". I think India should use it. Our offer on industrial goods and farm products was made against 18%, with zero duty on medicines and gems. If the final American rate is 17% plus a forced-labour duty, the offer should be weighed again.

The law firm Holland & Knight reports a senior US official saying more agreements could be finalised at the G20 summit in Miami on 14 and 15 December 2026. After that, comments on the proposed tariff are due by 5 January 2027 and hearings begin on 25 January 2027. India's other large negotiation, [the India-EU free trade agreement](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/india-eu-free-trade-agreement-explained.html), is further along. I think that gives our negotiators room to wait for a fair text.
