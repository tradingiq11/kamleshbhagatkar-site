The government has again pushed back the deadline for bids on India's deepwater and ultra-deepwater oil and gas blocks. The Directorate General of Hydrocarbons (DGH), which works under the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, posted the change on its website, and it was reported on Monday, 9 November. Bids for these blocks under the 10th and 11th rounds of the Open Acreage Licensing Policy (OALP-X and OALP-XI) are now due on 10 December. The earlier date was 15 November.

This is the seventh postponement for OALP-X and the third for OALP-XI. The DGH gave no reason. Industry sources quoted by the Millennium Post linked the delay to the Samudra Manthan National Offshore Exploration Scheme, whose operating guidelines came out on 22 October, and IANS reported the same link. The Millennium Post reported that the extra weeks give companies time to study the new support on offer, along with the geology of the blocks, the investment needed and the contract terms.

## What is on offer

OALP-X was launched in February 2025. It has 25 blocks covering about 1,82,589 square kilometres across 13 sedimentary basins, and at the time it was the largest round by area. Six blocks are onshore, six in shallow water, one in deep water and 12 in ultra-deep water. The 13 deepwater and ultra-deepwater blocks alone cover about 1,24,327 square kilometres.

OALP-XI was launched in March 2026 with 21 blocks covering 80,234.49 square kilometres across 11 basins: 12 onshore, four in shallow water, one in deep water and four in ultra-deep water.

Bidding for the onshore and shallow-water blocks in both rounds closed on 19 June 2026. Only the deep-sea blocks are still open. OALP-X was first due to close at the end of July 2025. It then moved to 31 October, 31 December, 18 February 2026 and 19 June 2026. For the deep-sea blocks, it then moved to 17 September, to 15 November and now to 10 December.

## Why Samudra Manthan matters

The Union Cabinet approved the Samudra Manthan scheme on 31 July 2026, with an outlay of Rs 84,084 crore up to 2030-31. Under the scheme, the government will meet up to 50 per cent of eligible deepwater exploration drilling costs, with a ceiling of Rs 675 crore per well. The scheme aims to add more than 600 million tonnes of oil equivalent to India's hydrocarbon resources.

The Millennium Post reported the main parts of the outlay: Rs 28,534 crore for offshore seismic data, Rs 43,200 crore for faster offshore exploration including 60 deepwater exploration wells, Rs 10,000 crore for common offshore infrastructure, and Rs 2,000 crore for oil and gas manufacturing and services zones.

When the scheme was approved, Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw called it "a very ambitious programme" and said, "A lot of foreign exchange will be saved from this." On the seismic surveys, he said: "Once we have proper data, then the exploration plan can be prepared properly."

## What it means for Bharat

I see the extension in a more positive light than the word "seventh" suggests. Deepwater exploration is expensive and risky. The scheme's cap of Rs 675 crore per well, for half the eligible cost, gives an idea of the sums a bidder is weighing. If the government has just changed the terms by offering to share half the drilling cost, it makes sense to give bidders time to price that in. A rushed round with weak bids would serve the country worse than a short delay.

But delays have a cost too. Every postponement pushes back the day a rig drills a well, and I think any production from these blocks is years away even in the best case. Seven postponements also send a signal to investors about how settled the terms are. I think the government's task now is to make 10 December a firm date.

The larger point is energy security. Vaishnaw said the scheme would save foreign exchange. In my view, every barrel found in our own waters is a barrel less exposed to wars and price shocks far from our shores. Most of the area on offer in OALP-X is offshore, and much of it is in deep and ultra-deep water, which is exactly where Samudra Manthan is aimed.

## What to watch next

The first thing to watch is whether bids are actually submitted on 10 December, or whether there is an eighth extension. After that, the number of bids for the 13 deep-sea blocks in OALP-X and the five in OALP-XI will show whether the support under Samudra Manthan has changed company decisions. I will also watch for the first contracts under the scheme for seismic surveys and the 60 exploration wells, because those will show whether the money is moving.
