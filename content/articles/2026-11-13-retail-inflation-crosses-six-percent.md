title: Retail Inflation Crosses 6 Per Cent: What October's Numbers Tell Us
date: 2026-11-13
category: Current Affairs
summary: Retail inflation rose to 6.09% in October, above the RBI's upper limit, driven by food. The RBI's policy committee meets on 2 to 4 December.
description: Kamlesh Bhagatkar explains India's October 2026 retail inflation of 6.09%, what drove it, what the RBI projected and what to watch at the December policy meeting.
place: New Delhi
---
India's retail inflation rose to 6.09 per cent in October, from 5.54 per cent in September. The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation released the figures on Thursday, 12 November. A Reuters poll had expected 5.93 per cent, so the number came in above forecasts. It is also above 6 per cent, the upper edge of the band the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is asked to keep inflation within. The target is 4 per cent, with 2 percentage points of tolerance on either side.

Food did most of the damage. Consumer food price inflation rose to 8.94 per cent from 7.74 per cent, ANI reported, with rural food inflation at 9.09 per cent and urban at 8.68 per cent. Onion prices were 116.22 per cent higher than a year earlier, up from 108.13 per cent in September. Garlic was up 60.74 per cent, ginger 48.32 per cent and lemons 47.30 per cent.

Villages are feeling it more than cities. Rural inflation rose to 6.63 per cent and urban inflation to 5.41 per cent. ThePrint noted that rural inflation has been higher than urban every month since February.

## Why the number climbed

The headline figure has risen all year. ThePrint reported that inflation was 2.74 per cent in January. By its count, inflation rose in October in 11 of the 12 groups in the index. Food and beverages, which make up 36.75 per cent of the basket, were 8.51 per cent dearer than a year ago, up from 7.36 per cent in September.

There is one detail in the data that I find reassuring. ThePrint showed that month-on-month price increases have slowed for four months in a row: 1.03 per cent in June, 0.89 per cent in July, 0.73 per cent in August, 0.69 per cent in September and 0.51 per cent in October. Prices are still rising, but more slowly. The annual figure keeps climbing mainly because prices a year ago were unusually flat, a low base.

ANI, citing the RBI's own assessment, linked the pressure to depleted stocks of stored onions and uneven rainfall that has disturbed the arrival of the fresh kharif crop. Oil is the other worry. When the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee met in October, its resolution spoke of "the consequent sharp volatility in crude oil prices" from the conflict in West Asia.

## What the RBI has already done

The RBI had expected something like this. At its meeting of 5 to 7 October, the committee voted unanimously to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent, and changed its stance from neutral to "calibrated tightening". Two members, Dr Nagesh Kumar and Prof Ram Singh, wanted the stance kept neutral, though both voted for the increase. The resolution said "inflation and its outlook are not benign as they were last year" and that "Food price increases have become more broad based". The minutes, published on 21 October, said "rate cuts are off the table in the near term".

The committee projected inflation at 6.0 per cent for October to December and 5.7 per cent for January to March, with 5.2 per cent for the full year. October's 6.09 per cent is close to that path. Under the rules, ThePrint explained, the RBI formally misses its target only if average inflation stays above 6 per cent for three consecutive quarters.

Economists expect another increase. Aditi Nayar, chief economist at ICRA, told ThePrint that a 25 basis point rise at the next meeting looks nearly certain, and that "the trajectory of crude oil prices, which have cooled in the recent sessions, will be critical" after that. She also warned that a "deficient monsoon and inadequate replenishment of reservoir levels" could hurt the rabi crop. Business Today reported that Madan Sabnavis of Bank of Baroda sees room for a rise of 25 or 50 basis points, while SBI Research, in its Ecowrap report of 12 November, cut its own forecast for December from 50 to 25 basis points. SBI pointed to a steadier rupee, foreign inflows of about $7.68 billion since 4 November and a fall of about 11 per cent in Brent crude after the US elections. It expects inflation of about 6.3 per cent in November before it eases.

## What it means for families

For a household in Nagpur, the numbers in the food basket matter more than the headline. A family that spends a large share of its income on vegetables and milk feels an 8.94 per cent food inflation rate more sharply than the 6.09 per cent average suggests, and the rural figures say that this is happening more in villages than in towns. Nayar told ThePrint that milk, sugar and onion prices are likely to keep food and beverages inflation above 8 per cent in November.

In my view, the RBI is right to treat this seriously but should not overreact. The monthly figures are slowing, and crude has fallen. A central bank that raises rates too far because of an onion shortage risks hurting growth that the RBI itself puts at 7.1 per cent this year. Interest rates can do little about a poor harvest. Supply measures, from releasing stocks to better storage, are where governments can do more.

## What to watch next

The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meets on 2 to 4 December. The November inflation figure will show whether SBI's estimate of about 6.3 per cent holds. And the rabi sowing season will show whether Nayar's warning about reservoirs comes true.
