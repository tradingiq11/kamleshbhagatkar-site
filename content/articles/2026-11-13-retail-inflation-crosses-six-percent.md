Retail inflation in India rose to 6.09 per cent in October, from 5.54 per cent in September. The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) released the provisional figures on Thursday, 12 November. That is above 6 per cent, the upper limit of the Reserve Bank of India's tolerance band. The RBI's target is 4 per cent, with a band of 2 to 6 per cent.

The Tribune, carrying ANI's report, called it a 24-month high. The last time the number was higher was October 2024, at 6.21 per cent. ThePrint reported that October was the ninth monthly rise in a row. In January, retail inflation stood at 2.74 per cent. Since then it has more than doubled.

Villages are feeling it more than cities. Rural inflation was 6.63 per cent in October and urban inflation 5.41 per cent. Food inflation climbed to 8.94 per cent from 7.74 per cent in September.

## Onions and the household budget

The figure that stands out is onion. Onion prices in October were 116.22 per cent higher than a year earlier, up from 108.13 per cent in September, according to The Tribune and Forbes India. In plain terms, onions cost more than twice what they did last October. Garlic was up 60.74 per cent, ginger 48.32 per cent and lemon 47.30 per cent. Potato went the other way, 14.24 per cent cheaper than a year ago.

The Tribune cited the RBI's view that supply-side pressures are behind food prices, including depleted stored onion stocks and uneven rainfall affecting the fresh kharif crop. Eating out has also become dearer. Inflation for restaurants and accommodation was 10.06 per cent.

The pressure is not limited to the kitchen. Aditi Nayar, chief economist at ICRA, said the rise was broad based, across 11 of the 12 divisions of the index, Forbes India reported. Seven states had headline inflation above 7 per cent, led by Tamil Nadu at 7.29 per cent, Telangana at 7.26 per cent and Andhra Pradesh at 7.25 per cent, according to the same report.

## What it means for the RBI and for borrowers

The RBI has already started to respond. At its meeting of 5 to 7 October, the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent and changed its stance from neutral to "calibrated tightening". The Committee said "inflationary pressures are no longer as benign as they were last year", according to SCC Online, and projected inflation of 6.0 per cent for the October to December quarter.

October's figure is already above that projection. ICRA expects inflation to average about 6.2 per cent in the quarter, with risks tilted to the upside, Forbes India reported. Nayar expects inflation to stay above the 6 per cent limit for the next couple of months, and she sees a further 25 basis point hike at the MPC meeting on 2 to 4 December as almost certain, ThePrint reported. Madan Sabnavis, chief economist at Bank of Baroda, sees the RBI choosing between 25 and 50 basis points, according to Forbes India.

I think the RBI will raise rates in December, and I think it has room to. Nayar expects GDP growth above 7 per cent in the July to September quarter, ThePrint reported. An economy growing at that pace can take a small rise in borrowing costs better than a family can take onions at double the price. I would expect banks to pass at least part of a hike on to borrowers over time.

But a rate hike is a slow tool for a supply problem. The repo rate cannot bring stored onions out of empty godowns or make a late rain fall on time. In my view, what it can do is stop high food prices from spreading into wages, rents and other prices. My reading is that the RBI is acting on that second risk, and that it is right to, since by Nayar's count the rise already covers 11 of the 12 divisions.

## What to watch next

The first test is the farm. Nayar flagged a deficient monsoon and low reservoir levels as risks to the rabi crop. Sabnavis pointed to lower sowing of rice, maize, oilseeds, cotton and sugarcane, and possibly weaker tur production, which he said could push prices up.

The second is the November inflation number. Nayar expects food inflation to stay above 8 per cent. If she is right, I expect the MPC's debate to centre on the size of the hike.

The third is the MPC meeting itself, on 2 to 4 December. Sabnavis also expects transport inflation to stay elevated, and Nayar said the path of crude oil prices will be critical for any further tightening in 2027.
