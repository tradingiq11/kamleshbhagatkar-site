Operation Sindoor was India's military response to the Pahalgam terror attack of 22 April 2025, in which 26 people were killed. In the early hours of 7 May 2025, the Indian armed forces struck nine terror sites in Pakistan and Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK). Three nights of exchanges followed, and the firing stopped at 5 pm on 10 May 2025, after Pakistan's Director General of Military Operations (DGMO) called his Indian counterpart.

That is nineteen days from the attack to the stoppage. I have set them out in order, from what the Government of India put on record in briefings, press releases and Parliament. Where Pakistan's account differs, I say so.

## What happened in Pahalgam on 22 April 2025?

Terrorists attacked tourists at Pahalgam in Jammu and Kashmir. Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri later gave the toll in one sentence: "They murdered 26 people, including one national of Nepal." The Resistance Front claimed responsibility. "This group is a front for the UN-proscribed Pakistani terrorist group, Lashkar-e-Taiba," Misri said.

Pakistan denies any role. Its National Security Committee said on 24 April 2025 that attempts to link Pakistan to the attack were "frivolous, devoid of rationality and defeat logic", according to PTI.

## What did India decide on 23 April 2025?

The Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS), chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, met the next day and was briefed on the cross-border linkages of the attack. Misri announced five decisions:

- The Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 "will be held in abeyance with immediate effect", until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably gives up its support for cross-border terrorism.
- The Integrated Check Post at Attari was closed with immediate effect.
- Pakistani nationals could no longer travel to India under the SAARC Visa Exemption Scheme. Those already in India on such visas had 48 hours to leave.
- The Defence, Military, Naval and Air Advisors in Pakistan's High Commission in New Delhi were declared persona non grata and given a week to leave. India withdrew its own advisors from Islamabad.
- The strength of the two High Commissions was to come down from 55 to 30 by 1 May 2025.

I have written separately about [where the Indus Waters Treaty stood in November 2026](https://kamleshbhagatkar.com/articles/2026-11-21-indus-treaty-pakistan-minister-delhi-sco.html).

## What happened between 24 April and 6 May 2025?

- **24 April 2025:** Pakistan's National Security Committee, chaired by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, answered with its own measures. PTI reported that it closed Pakistan's airspace to Indian airlines, shut the Wagah post, suspended all trade with India and said Pakistan would hold bilateral agreements, including the Simla Agreement, in abeyance. It said any attempt to stop or divert Pakistan's water "will be considered as an Act of war".
- **4 May 2025:** The Tribune reported that India had banned all direct and indirect imports from Pakistan, barred Pakistani-flagged ships from Indian ports, suspended postal services and closed Indian airspace to Pakistani carriers.

On 7 May 2025 Misri summed up these two weeks: "Despite a fortnight having passed since the attacks, there has been no demonstrable step from Pakistan" against terrorist infrastructure on its territory. He added that Indian intelligence indicated "further attacks against India were impending."

## What did India strike on 7 May 2025?

The Ministry of Defence announced the operation in a release posted at 1:44 am on 7 May 2025. It said nine sites had been targeted and that no Pakistani military facilities were hit. "Our actions have been focused, measured and non-escalatory in nature," the release said.

Later that day Misri briefed the press with Colonel Sofiya Qureshi and Wing Commander Vyomika Singh. As reported from that briefing, the strikes took place between 1:05 am and 1:30 am. The nine camps were:

- **In PoK:** the Sawai Nala and Syedna Bilal camps in Muzaffarabad, the Gulpur and Abbas camps in Kotli, and the Barnala camp in Bhimber.
- **In Pakistan:** the Sarjal and Mehmoona Joya camps in Sialkot, Markaz Taiba in Muridke and Markaz Subhanallah in Bahawalpur.

The briefing attributed them to Lashkar-e-Taiba, Jaish-e-Mohammed and Hizbul Mujahideen. "The locations were so selected to avoid damage to civilian infrastructures and loss of any civilian lives," the military briefing said, and "No military establishments were targeted."

Pakistan tells this differently. It said 31 civilians were killed in the strikes and claimed to have shot down five Indian fighter jets, Daily Sabah reported from agency copy. India rejected the first claim: Misri said on 8 May 2025 that "all attacks on the morning of 7th May were against carefully selected terrorist infrastructure." On the aircraft, he called the claims misinformation and said official information would come "when the time is right."

## What happened between 7 and 10 May 2025?

- **Night of 7 to 8 May 2025:** Wing Commander Singh said Pakistan "attempted to engage a number of military targets in Northern and Western India using drones and missiles", at 15 places from Avantipura and Srinagar to Bhuj, all neutralised by Indian air defence. On the morning of 8 May, India targeted air defence radars and systems in Pakistan, and an air defence system at Lahore was neutralised. "Indian response has been in the same domain, with the same intensity as Pakistan," Singh said. Misri said that since the morning of 7 May "a total of 16 civilians have been killed, and 59 others have been injured" on the Indian side, and that a gurdwara in Poonch had been hit.
- **Night of 8 to 9 May 2025:** Drone intrusions were "attempted from Leh to Sir Creek at 36 locations with approximately 300 to 400 drones", Singh said. Indian forces brought drones down by "kinetic and non-kinetic means". India launched armed drones at four air defence sites in Pakistan, and one destroyed a radar. Pakistan officially denied carrying out these attacks, a denial Misri called "blatantly farcical".
- **Night of 9 to 10 May 2025:** Singh said "limited damage was sustained to equipment and personnel at Indian Air Force stations" at Udhampur, Pathankot, Adampur and Bhuj. In response, "Pakistan military targets at Rafiqui, Murid, Chaklala, Rahim Yar Khan, Sukkur and Chunian were engaged", along with radar sites at Pasrur and the Sialkot aviation base. Pakistan claimed it had destroyed an S-400 system at Adampur, airfields at Suratgarh and Sirsa and a BrahMos base at Nagrota. "India unequivocally rejects these false narratives being spread by Pakistan," Singh said.

## How did the firing stop on 10 May 2025?

Misri announced it in a short statement. "The DGMO of Pakistan called the DGMO of India at 3.35 pm earlier this afternoon," he said. The two agreed that "both sides would stop all firing and military action on land, and in the air and sea" from 5 pm Indian Standard Time.

India's DGMO, Lieutenant General Rajiv Ghai, said Pakistan violated the arrangement within "a couple of hours", All India Radio reported on 12 May 2025. He sent a hotline message that further violations would be met "fiercely and punitively".

Who brought the stoppage about is disputed. US President Donald Trump said India and Pakistan had agreed to a "full and immediate" ceasefire after talks mediated by the United States. Shehbaz Sharif wrote: "We thank President Trump for his leadership and proactive role for peace in the region." India's account is that the two militaries settled it directly. External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar told the Lok Sabha on 28 July 2025: "Between April 22 and June 17, there was no call between Donald Trump and Prime Minister Modi."

## What did India say it hit, and why?

India's stated reason is in Misri's statement of 7 May 2025: "India exercised its right to respond and pre-empt as well as deter more such cross-border attacks."

By the official account, the nine sites of 7 May were terror camps, and Pakistani air defence sites and air bases were engaged only afterwards, in reply to Pakistan's attacks on Indian military stations. I think this order is the most important fact in the timeline, because it shows who escalated at each step.

The casualty figures are India's own. Lieutenant General Ghai said the strikes on the nine camps "killed over 100 terrorists" and that the Pakistan Army "lost approximately 35 to 40 personnel" in firing on the Line of Control between 7 and 10 May 2025. Pakistan's own figure is 31 civilians dead in the strikes. I have found no neutral count.

## Where do things stand?

The Prime Minister told the nation on 12 May 2025 that the operation was paused and could resume: "we have just suspended our retaliatory action against Pakistan's terror and military camps." He added: "India will not tolerate any nuclear blackmail", and "Water and blood cannot flow together." Any talks with Pakistan, he said, would be on terrorism and on PoK.

On 29 July 2025, Home Minister Amit Shah told the Lok Sabha that the three terrorists who carried out the Pahalgam attack had been killed in Operation Mahadev in Jammu and Kashmir the previous day.

Two things would change the present position. One is Pakistan meeting India's condition on cross-border terrorism, which would reopen the question of the treaty. The other is a fresh attack, which under the policy of 12 May 2025 would end the pause.
