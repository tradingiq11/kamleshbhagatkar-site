# Writing rules for kamleshbhagatkar.com articles

Binding for every article. Author: Kamlesh Bhagatkar, author and geopolitical analyst from Nagpur.
Voice samples: the three essays already in `content/articles/` (dated 2026-11-09). Match their sentence length, plainness and first-person tone.

## Voice
- First person ("I") as an Indian writer, calm, confident and analytical. Use "we/our" only for India as a nation.
- Written from Bharat's point of view: explain what an event means for India's security, economy and standing, and answer hostile claims with facts.
- Indian English spelling (defence, programme, neighbour). Use lakh and crore where the source does. Say "Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK)".
- Plain words, varied sentence length, short paragraphs. Readers are educated non-specialists: business owners, students, families of soldiers.
- One clear argument per article. Open with the concrete news, not with background. Close on the last concrete fact or a specific thing to watch, not a slogan.

## Structure (750 to 1,000 words)
1. Two or three paragraphs: what happened, when, who said what (with numbers).
2. Two or three sections with `##` headings in sentence case: context, what it means for Bharat, what to watch next.
3. `## Sources`: every source used, as `- [Outlet: headline](url)`.

## Sourcing (non-negotiable)
- Every fact, number, date, name and quote comes from a source you opened during research. Quotes are verbatim.
- A key claim needs two independent sources, or one primary source (PIB, MEA, a ministry, RBI, ISRO, Parliament, a court order, the UN, a foreign government).
- Where accounts differ, say so and name who says what.
- Opinions must read as opinions ("I think", "I read this as"). Never present an opinion as a fact.
- Claims about any named living person must be attributed ("said", "according to", "alleged").

## Red lines: never write
- Anything defamatory, or accusations against a named person that the sources do not attribute.
- Slurs, mockery or generalisations about any religion, community, caste or nationality, including Pakistani people or Muslims. Criticise states, groups and policies, never communities.
- Party-political attacks, election predictions, or support for or against a party or candidate. Government policy may be analysed; partisanship may not.
- Cases still being heard in court (sub judice): report only what a court has ordered or said.
- Crime stories about private individuals, victims' names, or anything about minors.
- Breaking news under 6 hours old with only one source; casualty figures not from an official source.
- Military operational details beyond what official briefings released.
- Unreleased content from the author's book *Nineteen Days*. It may be mentioned only as "my next book, *Nineteen Days*, on Pahalgam and Operation Sindoor".
- Medical, legal or investment advice.

## Style (see `HUMANIZER.md` for the full list)
- No em or en dashes. Use commas, colons, parentheses or full stops. Hyphens in compound words are fine.
- No "not X but Y" / "not just / not only" contrasts unless they correct a belief readers really hold.
- No one-line dramatic closers, no forced triads, no staged openers ("Here's the thing", "Let's dive in").
- Banned words: pivotal, testament, tapestry, delve, crucial, vibrant, landscape (abstract), underscore, showcase, enduring, robust (figurative), meticulous, intricate, "stands as", "serves as", "plays a key role", "in today's world".
- Headings in sentence case, no emojis, no bold labels.
